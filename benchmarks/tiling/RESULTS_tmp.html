Tile extraction: slideflow vs. slideforge, results

Status: experimental (exp/slideforge-tiling). Measured: 2026-11-07/08.

This document summarizes the results of a full benchmark run comparing the two tiling backends (default slideflow and experimental slideforge) with regards to speed, memory usage and tile quality. Preliminary evaluation indicate that slideforge is the faster option while featuring similar tile quality, although these are not definitive results (see [Limitations])

Summary

  • Speed: slideforge was faster in 11 of 12 settings, by 1.2–3.5×. Comparing each backend's fastest setting, it took 3.6 s instead of 11.1 s (High resolution, tissue detection on).
  • CPU: slideforge used 1.6–3.7× less CPU time in all 12 settings.
  • Scaling: slideflow seems to barely speed up beyond 4 threads. slideforge kept getting faster up to 16 threads.
  • Same tiles: without tissue detection, both produce the same grid (Jaccard 1.0) with nearly identical pixels (mean SSIM ≥ 0.99). With tissue detection, 94–96% of tiles agree at the calibrated min_tissue_fraction=0.4.
  • Exception: at Low resolution with tissue detection on and one thread, slideflow was faster (19.6 s vs. 24.4 s), because it used about 2 cores even with num_threads=1.
  • Not tested yet: Differences in stain normalization (on by default in AutoMIL), whether models trained on slideforge tiles perform differently, and slide formats other than SVS.

Methods

The new Tiling module exposes two classes representing the tiling backends which both feature a .extract serving as the entry point for tile extraction. The desired backend can be selected with Dataset(tiler=TileBackendType.…) or the (currently hidden) --tiler CLI option, and both write slideflow-compatible tfrecords and index files. Downstream tasks such as training and evaluation are performed indentically.

slideflow (default) slideforge (experimental)

Tissue detection

Both detect tissue in two stages: once on a low-resolution overview of the whole slide, and once per tile.

slideflow (qc="otsu") slideforge (min_tissue_fraction)
Overview Smallest pyramid level with shorter side > 500 px Lowest pyramid level
Signal HSV saturation, 7×7 median blur Grayscale, no blur
Threshold One Otsu threshold per slide, tissue is above it One Otsu threshold per slide, tissue is at or below it
Stage 1 (overview, before reading at full resolution) Drop a tile if more than 60% of it is background Drop a tile if less than min_tissue_fraction of it is tissue
Stage 2 (each decoded tile) Grayspace filter: drop if more than 60% of pixels have saturation < 0.05 Tissue fraction recomputed at full resolution with the same threshold

Both rules keep a tile with at least 40% tissue, which matches the calibrated min_tissue_fraction of 0.4. The two will never select exactly the same tiles, because they threshold different signals.

Software Python 3.12.3, slideflow 3.0.2 (cucim 26.10), slideforge 37773ea, AutoMIL f3edb49 + this branch

Each run uses a fresh subprocess and slideflow project. Pending disk writes are flushed before each run. The page cache is not dropped, so slides are read from a warm cache. Times are wall-clock time of the extraction call (including index writing for slideforge). Memory is the peak PSS of the process tree, which includes about 1 GB from importing slideflow.

Notes:

  • Single-thread runs: at Low resolution, slideflow used 2.0–2.8 cores even with num_threads=1, so those times aren't single-core times. The CPU time ratio is the fairer comparison there, and it favours slideforge in every setting.
  • Memory: slideforge's peak was lower in every 16-thread setting (1.1–1.6 GB vs. 1.9–2.5 GB). It was higher in four of the single- and four-thread settings, by up to 0.3 GB.
  • Disk usage: slideforge's tfrecords were 2–6% larger (e.g. 740 vs. 701 MB at High with tissue detection).
  • Disk stalls: 4 of 72 runs spent more than 5% of their time waiting on writes (WSL virtual-disk write stalls, measured with Linux pressure stall information). The medians above include them. The affected settings are slideforge at High / off / 16 and, once each, slideflow at High / on / 16 and Low / on / 4.

Tile equivalence

Compared with compare.py, which matches tiles by location and computes pixel similarity on 50 tiles per slide.

Tile overlap (Jaccard) Mean SSIM of matched tiles
Tissue detection off 1.00 on all three slides 0.991–0.996
Tissue detection on, min_tissue_fraction=0.4 (overall) 0.94 (High), 0.96 (Low) —
Tissue detection on, min_tissue_fraction=0.5 (per slide, before calibration) 0.87–0.98 0.980–0.982

The pixel differences without tissue detection (mean absolute difference 0.4–0.9 of 255) come from the two implementations' different resizing and JPEG encoding.

Calibrating min_tissue_fraction

calibrate.py compares slideforge's tile selection at different min_tissue_fraction values with slideflow's (Jaccard over all three slides):

min_tissue_fraction 0.3 0.35 0.4 0.45 0.5
High 0.913 0.933 0.942 0.931 0.900
Low 0.923 0.956 0.956 0.911 0.858

0.4 was the best value at both resolutions (at Low narrowly: 0.9561 vs. 0.9557 for 0.35) and also won every leave-one-slide-out split, within 0.01. It's only calibrated on these three slides. Rerun calibrate.py on a sample of a new dataset before relying on it.

Limitations

  • Limited dataset size: The benchmark has been computed on 3 slides only from the OpenSlide test data. Subsequent benchmark runs should be computed on full WSI datasets.

  • Differences stain normalization. While both backends utilized reinhard normalization, the two implementations use different target colours, and are therefore not fully equivalent. Slideforges implementation could be revised to match slideflows implementation more closely.

  • No downstream evaluation. The full AutoMIL pipeline has not been run usng the experimental backend, providing no insight on whether models trained on the different tile sets perform better or worse.

  • WSL2 environment. The experiment was run on a Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL) with an Ubuntu 24.04 distro and is not representative of a native Linux environment.

  • SVS only. slideforge can't read the other formats slideflow supports.

Reproducing

pip install -e ".[slideforge]"
python benchmarks/tiling/bench.py --slides <slides> --out <out> \
    --resolution High --resolution Low --threads 1 --threads 4 --threads 16 --repeats 3
python benchmarks/tiling/calibrate.py --slides <slides> --out <out>/calibration --resolution High --resolution Low
